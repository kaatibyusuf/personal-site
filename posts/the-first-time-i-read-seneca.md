---
title: the first time i read seneca
date: 2026-11-24
summary: a small book asked me a question i couldn't answer, and left me with hope instead of guilt.
---
the first time i read *on the shortness of life* by seneca, i finished it in one sitting and then just sat there. it's a small book. you can read it in an afternoon. but it asked me a question i didn't have an answer for.

his point is simple, and a bit rude. he says life is not short. we just waste most of it. we give our days away to things we don't even want, and then complain that time ran out.

i felt caught. i thought about all the days i had called "busy" that weren't really mine. days spent on noise, on people's opinions, on tomorrow. i was always about to start living, and always a bit late.

but this is the part i want to write about, because it's not a sad book to me. it's a hopeful one.

if the problem is that we waste time, then the problem is something we can fix. it's not fate. it's not that i was born late or slow or behind. it's habits. it's choices. and those can change, even a little, even today.

that's what growth is, i think. it's not a big dramatic day where everything changes. it's choosing one thing today that you won't regret tomorrow. then doing it again.

and hope is what makes you start. hope says the person i'm trying to become is not a fantasy. hope says i'm allowed to begin again, even if i've wasted a lot. as a muslim, i feel this strongly. the door of return is always open. you don't have to fix your whole past before you take one step forward.

so that's why i'm starting this blog. i do not claim to have it figured out. i don't. i'm writing to remember what i'm trying to become, and maybe to remind you too.

if you're reading this and you feel late, you're not too late. start small. start today.
