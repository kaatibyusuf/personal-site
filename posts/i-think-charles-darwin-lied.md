summary: a critique of the law of natural selection, from darwin's own words, and what a muslim should make of it.
---
> "This preservation of favourable variations and the rejection of injurious variations, I call Natural Selection." (charles darwin, *on the origin of species*, 1859)

that sentence is the whole engine. everything taught in school as "evolution" hangs on it. i grew up being told it was a law, the same way gravity is a law. so i want to ask a simple question: is it?

first, a word about the title. i am not claiming to read darwin's heart. i can't prove he knew he was wrong and wrote anyway. what i can show, from his own books and letters, is that he wrote with more certainty than his evidence allowed, he admitted the weak points in private and then moved past them in public, and the world after him turned a hypothesis into a creed. you can call that a lie or you can call it overreach. i call it something that deserves to be examined, and i think a muslim who has never examined it has been handed a conclusion without the evidence.

so let me do it properly, from his own text, and then let me be fair to the other side at the end.

## 1. it is not a law

a law in science predicts. you drop a stone, you know how fast it falls. you can write it as an equation and be wrong in public.

natural selection, as darwin stated it, is an observation about what survives. "the fit survive" is true of every outcome, because whatever survived is, by definition, what we call fit. ask what makes an animal fit and the answer is that it survived. ask why it survived and the answer is that it was fit. the circle closes without touching the ground.

the philosopher karl popper made this charge in the 1970s, and he later softened it. so i will say it fairly: modern biologists have tried to rescue the idea by measuring fitness through reproduction and writing models for it. those models are real. but they work on traits that already exist inside a population that already exists. they tell you how a trait spreads. they do not tell you where the trait came from. a principle that only describes what happens to things that already exist is a useful principle. it is not a law of origins.

## 2. selection does not create anything

read darwin's sentence again. selection preserves. it rejects. it is a filter. a filter can only remove what is put before it.

darwin knew this. he wrote that natural selection can do nothing unless profitable variations occur. so the real question is where the new, useful, complicated things come from. and in 1859 darwin had no answer. he knew nothing about genes. his own theory of inheritance, which he called pangenesis, was wrong, and he leaned on the old idea that use and disuse of an organ is passed down to children. in 1867 fleeming jenkin pointed out that if traits blend in the offspring, any useful variation would be diluted away within a few generations. darwin conceded the problem troubled him.

the answer arrived decades later through genetics, and it is mutation: random copying errors. here is where the critique bites. most mutations are harmful or do nothing. the japanese geneticist motoo kimura argued in 1968 that most of the change we see at the molecular level is neutral, not selected at all. that is the mainstream position now. so the picture of selection as the great sculptor, carving species out of raw variation, is much weaker than the one that was sold to us. a filter working on random errors, most of which are neutral, is being asked to produce an eye, a wing, a brain.

## 3. darwin set his own test, and then argued around it

he wrote this himself:

> "If it could be demonstrated that any complex organ existed, which could not possibly have been formed by numerous, successive, slight modifications, my theory would absolutely break down."

and about the eye:

> "To suppose that the eye... could have been formed by natural selection, seems, I freely confess, absurd in the highest possible degree."

then he went on to argue that it is not absurd, that a chain of small steps from a light-sensitive patch to a camera eye is imaginable. in a letter to the american botanist asa gray, he admitted that the sight of a peacock's feather made him sick, because it did not fit.

i want to be fair. his answer has been developed since. in 1994 two swedish researchers, dan-eric nilsson and susanne pelger, modelled how an eye could evolve from a flat patch of cells in a very large number of generations. but look at what that is: a computer model that assumes each small step is useful and heritable. it shows the path is not mathematically impossible. it does not show that it happened. and darwin's test was never "is it imaginable?" his test was "can it be demonstrated?" an imagined path is not a demonstrated one.

## 4. the record was supposed to be full of the steps, and it was not

darwin asked the question himself, in his own chapter on difficulties:

> "Why, if species have descended from other species by insensibly fine gradations, do we not everywhere see innumerable transitional forms?"

his answer was that the geological record is extremely imperfect, and that the fossils would be found later. on the sudden appearance of the major animal groups in the cambrian rocks, he wrote that he could give no satisfactory answer.

a hundred and sixty years later we have found many fossils. that is true and i won't pretend otherwise. but the pattern the digging revealed was not the smooth gradual slope darwin predicted. species tend to appear suddenly, stay almost unchanged for millions of years, and then disappear. this was so persistent that in 1972 two evolutionary biologists, niles eldredge and stephen jay gould, proposed punctuated equilibrium, a revision which says change happens in short bursts. notice what that means. when the evidence did not fit the original picture, the picture was changed to fit the evidence, and the theory stayed standing. a theory that survives every pattern is a theory worth asking hard questions about.

## 5. it says nothing about the beginning

darwin's book is about how species change once life exists. life itself, the first self-copying cell with its coded information and its machinery, is outside the theory. he acknowledged this privately, speculating about a warm little pond. today the origin of life is still an open problem, and no accepted account explains how chemistry became code. so when the public hears "science has explained where we came from", it is being told something larger than what the theory covers.

## 6. what he said about people

i don't think a theory is false because its author said ugly things. that would be a bad argument and i won't make it. but the words matter for another reason: they show where the idea went when it was applied to man. in *the descent of man*, darwin wrote:

> "At some future period, not very distant as measured by centuries, the civilised races of man will almost certainly exterminate, and replace, the savage races throughout the world."

he wrote this as a prediction, not a wish, and in the same book he praised sympathy and wrote against cruelty. both are true. but within a generation, the language of struggle and fitness was being used to justify eugenics and racial hierarchies, by people who thought they were following the science.

this is the point where a believer has something to say that a biologist cannot. if man is only a product of selection, the strong version of the story gives no reason that a human being has dignity as such. it gives you only what survived. the qur'an gives the opposite foundation:

> "and we have certainly honoured the children of adam." (al-isrā' 17:70)

dignity here is not earned by being fit. it is given, to every human being, by the one who made him.

## 7. adam, alayhis-salam

and this is where it touches our deen directly. Allah says:

> "indeed, the example of Jesus to Allah is like that of adam. he created him from dust; then he said to him, 'be,' and he was." (āl ʿimrān 3:59)

the story the qur'an tells is a specific creation of a specific man, honoured, taught the names, and made a khalifah. the majority position among our scholars through the centuries has been that adam was created directly and not descended from earlier beings. i should be honest that some contemporary muslim scholars discuss how far common descent can be accepted for other creatures, and that is a real discussion, not one to wave away. but the claim that man is only a branch on a tree of accidents does not sit beside this verse. a muslim has to choose what he is willing to hold, and he should choose knowing what he is choosing.

## now, to be fair

i said i would be fair, and an essay that only argues one side is a sermon, not an inquiry. so here is what a serious biologist would answer, and i don't think it is weak.

the genetics problem was solved. mendel's work, rediscovered in 1900, and then the modern synthesis in the 1930s and 40s, gave selection exactly the inheritance mechanism darwin lacked. the mathematics of population genetics, by fisher, haldane and wright, makes selection quantitative and testable. natural selection can be measured in real populations, and it has been: bacteria evolving resistance to antibiotics, finches changing beak size in a drought, insects becoming immune to pesticides. no one doubts that part. the real dispute is whether such small changes, extended over millions of years, can account for everything from bacteria to birds.

on that, they would point to the fossils found since darwin: tiktaalik, a fish with the beginnings of limbs, and the series of whale ancestors with shrinking hind legs. they would point to dna, where the genetic code is shared across all life, where humans carry the same broken genes as other primates in the same places, and where the family tree built from genes matches the one built from bones. these lines of evidence were not available to darwin, and they point the same direction. biologists would also say that the cambrian "explosion" took tens of millions of years, and that punctuated equilibrium is a refinement within evolution, not a retreat from it.

and on the eye, they would say we can see the whole range of eyes still living today, from the light-sensing patch to the camera eye, which shows each step works.

i don't think these answers are silly, and the great majority of scientists, including many who are religious, accept them. what i'm saying is narrower. the claim that evolution has been demonstrated from first cell to man is a bigger claim than the observations, and the people who tell it that way often skip the difficulties darwin himself listed. he was more honest about them than many who came after him.

## where i stand

that is why i titled this the way i did. darwin wrote a long book, full of real observation and real doubt, and the world received it as a closed case. the doubts did not survive the retelling. a student is told that evolution is as certain as gravity, and he never meets the man who wrote "absurd in the highest possible degree".

so read the origin itself. read the chapter on difficulties. read what the critics said then and what the defenders say now. and above all, don't let anyone, a teacher, a textbook or a scientist in a white coat, tell you that you must give up what the revelation tells you about who you are before they have shown you every step of the proof.

seek the truth wherever it leads, and ask Allah to guide you to it.
