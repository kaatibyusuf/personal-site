do you really want it?

28 November 2026 · 5 min read

i love animals. if dogs were allowed in islam, i would have gotten one. regardless, i have rabbits, chickens and a cat named humayyah.

i start with them because they are the best teachers of wanting that i know. and because there is a question i have been carrying, and i want to put it to you the way an older man puts a question to a younger one on a long road.

you say you want success. do you really want it?

don't answer quickly because most people do in one breath, and that is the problem. a thing that is truly wanted is never said in one breath. it is paid for, slowly, in days nobody sees.

the wish and the want

you will say: but i do want it. i think about it every day.

thinking about a thing is not the same as wanting it. a man who wants bread does not sit thinking about bread. he gets up and bakes. a wish is something you hold. a want is something that moves your hands.

seneca says, in one of his letters, that no wind is favourable to a sailor who does not know which harbour he is sailing to. i think about this whenever i hear someone say "i want to be successful". that is a sailor shouting that he wants wind. wind to where?

success is not a harbour. it is a word we use so we don't have to decide. success at what? by whose measure? to be seen, or to be useful? to be rich, or to be free? these are not the same road, and some of them run in opposite directions. a man who has not chosen will spend his best years walking half of one road, then half of another, and call it ambition.

so before anything else, name the harbour. say it in one plain sentence, one a child could understand. if you can't, you don't yet want success. you want to feel like someone who has it.

what the rabbits know

my rabbits eat all day. there's always more, and they are never full. and every shadow, every sudden sound, sends them running into a corner.

i see in them the man who wants everything. always eating, never fed, always afraid. his hunger has no direction, so it can never be satisfied. seneca writes that it is not the man who has too little who is poor, but the man who craves more. the rabbit has a full bowl and a frightened heart.

watch yourself for this. if every new thing makes you hungry, a new course, a new business, a new person's success, you don't have too little. you have an appetite with no harbour.

what the chickens know

the chickens have no idea what a market is. they have never heard of a strategy. each day they do the same small things: scratch the ground, peck, wander, lay. no one claps for an egg. and yet, if you wait, there is a basket.

we love the story of the basket. we skip the scratching.

nearly everyone who tells me they want success wants the basket. very few want the scratching, which is the real work: small, repeated, unseen, a little boring. but the basket is only the scratching, added up.

ask yourself honestly: if no one ever saw it, would i still do the work? would i do it for ten years, in secret, with no applause? if the answer is no, then you don't want the success. you want the applause. and applause is a different thing, much cheaper to get and much easier to lose.

what humayyah knows

my cat does not ask anyone how she's doing. she doesn't compare herself to the neighbour's cat. when she wants something she can sit for an hour without moving, completely still, completely awake.

this is what patience really is. it is not sleeping. it is tension held in stillness. and notice that she doesn't chase everything. she picks one thing and waits for it. most of us chase ten mice and catch none, then say the world is unfair.

wanting without noise is the highest form of wanting. the loud kind, the kind that announces itself and posts about it, burns out its heat on talk.

the price

everything has a price. nothing worth having is free, and anything that looks free is just being paid for somewhere you can't see yet.

so don't ask only "what do i want?" ask "what am i willing to pay?" you will have to pay with sleep, with comfort, with being misunderstood, with watching other people move faster for a while, with saying no to pleasant things. if you look at that bill and still say yes, then you want it. if you flinch, that's not a failure. it's information. it's cheaper to find it out now than at forty.

and the half that isn't yours

there is one more thing, and it keeps you sane.

the stoics said that some things are up to us and some are not. our deen says it with more tenderness. a man once asked the prophet ﷺ whether he should tie his camel and trust in Allah, or leave it untied and trust. he said: tie it, and trust. it's reported in the tirmidhi.

do your part fully. tie the camel properly. work, plan, practise, be honest, be patient. then let go of the result, because the result was never in your hands. a man who clings to the result lives in fear. a man who does his work and leaves the rest to his lord can sleep.

questions to carry

if you're going to keep one thing from this letter, keep these:

  • what exactly is my harbour, in one plain sentence?
  • am i hungry like the rabbit, or working like the chicken?
  • would i still do this if no one ever knew?
  • what am i willing to pay, and have i looked at the bill?
  • have i tied my camel, or am i only hoping?

be well, my friend. want slowly. and want truly.
